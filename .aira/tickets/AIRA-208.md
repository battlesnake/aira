confine's `cpu-weight=aging` carries no number, so the weight a job ran at (100→10 over 30 min) is observable on no AIRA surface, and a mistyped schedule is discarded silently
**kind** bug · **severity** P2 · **closes** RANT-32

**SYMPTOM.** Every confine scope decays cpu.weight 100→10 over 30 minutes, so a long job runs at a live 10:1 disadvantage against a fresh sibling. Verified live 2026‑09‑09: `aira.slice` simultaneously held scopes at weight 10 (39 m integration-gate), 10 (36 m), 20 (10 m48 s) and 100 (fresh), with `cpu` in `aira.slice/cgroup.subtree_control` — the 39 m merge gate taking 10/140 = 7 % of contested CPU against the fresh job's 71 %. None of that is visible from AIRA. RANT-32 attributes nine >300 s pytest timeouts to this; the mechanism is arithmetically capable of it, **but nobody can confirm or refute the attribution because the number was never recorded anywhere**. That is the actual cost: a shipped behaviour that cannot be measured after the fact, and triage hours spent on an unfalsifiable hypothesis. Second face: a mistyped `AIRA_CONFINE_CPUWEIGHT_SCHEDULE` is discarded and the job runs the compiled default while the trailer still says `aging`.

**ROOT CAUSE.** `internal/runner/confine.go:956-960` renders `" cpu-weight=" + string(cpuWeight)` where `ConfineCPUWeight` is the two-valued enum `aging|unavailable` (`confine.go:182-183`); the JSON field `cpu_weight` (`confine.go:277`) carries the same enum; `aira confine --list` has no weight column. The config (`Start`, `Floor`, `Steps`) is computed at arming (`confine_linux.go:810-816`) and thrown away; `startCPUWeightDecay` (`:2174-2205`) writes each step to sysfs and records nothing back. `confine_linux.go:2087-2091` is `if parsed, err := parseCPUWeightSchedule(raw); err == nil { steps = parsed }` — the error is dropped with no diagnostic and no change to the reported facet. (Two adjacent behaviours are deliberate and documented in-code and are **not** bugs to fix here: the final step overwritten by the floor at `:2106`, comment `:2103-2105`; and `parseCPUWeightEnv`'s silent fallback/clamp at `:2110-2120`.)

**PROPOSED FIX.** Stay inside the existing facet discipline. (1) Carry the installed curve's endpoints and render `cpu-weight=aging(100->10@30m)`, keeping the enum as the leading token so existing parsers and the ci-shim `unevaluated` path are untouched. (2) Name a rejected schedule — `...,config-rejected` plus one line on the supervisor's diagnostics stream — while keeping the fail-safe fallback to the compiled default exactly as the Slice-1 plan decided ("never inert-by-accident"); only the silence changes. (3) Document, next to the other confine facets: that aging exists and what the default curve is; that weight arbitrates **between** scopes only (xdist workers inside one scope share equally regardless); and that `AIRA_CONFINE_CPUWEIGHT_FLOOR=100` disables aging outright — a working kill-switch documented nowhere outside the plan spec and the source. **Deferred:** a `CPU-WT` column on `confine --list`, and any change to whether or how fast a job ages.

**HOW TO TEST.** A `FormatConfineStatus` unit asserting the rendered line contains `cpu-weight=aging(100->10@30m)` (fails today — bare enum; revert the renderer and it fails again, so it is discriminating). `confineCPUWeightConfig` with `SCHEDULE="garbage"` must report the rejection **and** still return the compiled default steps — the first half fails today, the second pins the fail-safe so the fix cannot regress into a refusal. `SCHEDULE="1s:100,2s:50"` with no FLOOR must report the **installed** curve (ending at 10, per `:2106`), pinning the trailer to what was written to sysfs. A real-cgroup test with `FLOOR=100` asserting `<scope>/cpu.weight` stays 100 across a step boundary.
